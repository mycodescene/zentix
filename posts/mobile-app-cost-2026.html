What a mobile app really costs in 2026

2026-10-23 · 7 min read

Ask five companies what an app costs and you'll get five wildly different numbers. That's because "an app" can mean anything from a simple booking tool to a full marketplace. Here's what actually drives the price.

The real cost drivers

It's not the screens; it's the complexity behind them. User accounts and login are cheap. Real-time chat, payments, maps, and admin dashboards each add meaningful cost. The biggest driver of all is integrations: every external system your app talks to is design, development, and testing work.

iOS, Android, or both?

Building two native apps roughly doubles the cost of one. Cross-platform frameworks like React Native and Flutter changed this math: one codebase, both stores, typically 60 to 70 percent of the cost of two native apps. For most business apps, cross-platform is the right call. Only games and extremely performance-sensitive apps truly need native.

The quote red flags

Be wary of quotes that are dramatically cheaper than the rest. They usually mean one of three things: the scope is misunderstood, corners will be cut on testing and security, or the price will quietly double halfway through. A trustworthy quote breaks down features individually and explains what's excluded.

What to budget beyond the build

The build is only the beginning. Budget for app store fees, server and database hosting, ongoing maintenance, and OS updates. A good rule: plan 15 to 20 percent of the build cost per year for maintenance. Apps that aren't maintained rot fast.

The bottom line

Start smaller than you think. The most successful apps we've seen launched with a tight core feature set, proved the concept with real users, then grew. A focused first version beats an ambitious one that never ships.
